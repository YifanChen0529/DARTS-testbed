\begin{lemma}[Per-node travel eccentricity]
\label{lem:s1-eccentricity}
Under the straight-line travel-time instantiation of the offline envelope, with
agents confined to the workspace $W = [0, L]^2$ throughout execution, define
\[
\hat\tau_v
  := \max_{c \in \mathrm{vert}(W)}
     \frac{\|c - \mathbf{pos}_v\|}{u_{\max}}.
\]
Then every travel leg toward $v$, whether from an initial pose, from a
predecessor task endpoint in a recomposed chain, or after a preemptive goal
redirection, has straight-line travel time at most $\hat\tau_v$. Replacing
$\bar\tau$ by $\hat\tau_v$ in the per-node envelope of the offline path-sum
test preserves the upper-bound property, and the resulting test is sound
whenever the baseline instantiation's travel model is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every leg origin lies in $W$: initial poses are sampled in $W$; task endpoints
lie in $W$; intermediate positions remain in $W$ by position clipping; and a
mid-leg redirection origin is a convex combination of points in $W$, hence
again in $W$. The map $p \mapsto \|p - \mathbf{pos}_v\|$ is convex on $W$, so
its maximum over the polytope $W$ is attained at a vertex. That vertex maximum
is $\hat\tau_v \cdot u_{\max}$, and dividing by $u_{\max}$ bounds the
straight-line travel time of any leg toward $v$. Soundness of the path-sum then
follows from componentwise monotonicity of the test in the per-node envelopes,
by the same lifting argument as Remark~2.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
On the four registered DBench-4 geometries, $\hat\tau_v \le \bar\tau$ for every
node, so the refined test is uniformly at least as tight there. For arbitrary
task placements the two instantiations are incomparable, because the baseline
fixes the start set to a single parking corner while $\hat\tau_v$ covers
arbitrary initial poses in $W$.
\end{remark}
